Burn–Coast–Burn Deorbit Simulation

Demonstrate active deorbit control using a high-specific-impulse thruster, transitioning from the initial state to LEO.

Key Topics

  • Modeling gravitational two-body motion and understanding orbital energy
  • Physics-based thrust modeling (Isp, thrust, mass flow rate)
  • Sequencing maneuvers (burn → coast → burn) and deorbit strategy

Assumptions

  • Gravitational Model: Two-body system; excludes drag, J₂, solar/lunar perturbations.
  • Propulsion: Constant retrograde thrust (6 N), Isp = 5000 s, g₀ = 9.807 m/s².
  • Initial State: r₀ = [26578, 0, 0] km, v₀ = [0, 3.8726, 0] km/s, mass = 600 kg.

Burn–Coast–Burn Sequence

  • Burn 1: 3 days of continuous thrust
  • Coast: 4 days with no thrust
  • Burn 2: 1 day of continuous thrust

Retrograde Thrust

  • Efficiently reduces orbital velocity, lowering orbital energy.
  • Lowers perigee without wasting fuel on non-productive velocity changes.

Results

  • Fuel used: 42.2882 kg
  • Final altitude: 251.522 km

The vertical lines mark the three mission phases. The first 3-day burn causes a sharp altitude drop. The coast phase is flat with orbital oscillations under gravity only. The final 1-day burn shows a smaller descent—thanks to reduced mass and duration. This demonstrates thrust altering orbital energy versus passive gravitational motion.
